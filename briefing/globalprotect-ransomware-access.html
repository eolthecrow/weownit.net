From VPN to ransomware: review GlobalProtect access

Unit 42 research updated in October connects VPN exploitation with ransomware and access to virtual infrastructure.

weownit.net editorial analysis · weownit.net publication: 2026-10-26 · Reviewed: 2026-10-26

Topic

GlobalProtect · PAN-OS · VPN · Virtualization

Original source

Unit 42 — updated research ↗ · Palo Alto Networks PSIRT ↗

In focus

Exploitation reported · Research updated in October

What was reported

Unit 42 reports ransomware operators, including Qilin and INC, using CVE-2026-0257. The vulnerability was published in May; the October research update describes evolving activity.

Applicability and impact

Check the PAN-OS version and the Authentication Override/certificate combination specified by PSIRT. Not every GlobalProtect configuration has the same exposure. If a VPN is compromised, investigation also covers systems reachable from its zone.

What to check and remediate

  1. Check: compare the version and portal/gateway settings with the official advisory. Record the result and who can change the configuration.
  2. Contain: apply the vendor’s temporary mitigation if an update is not yet possible; test the authentication impact and preserve recovery administration access.
  3. Remediate: plan an upgrade to a fixed release for your branch, with backup, rollback and a VPN reconnection test. Do not assume enabling MFA alone fixes the bypass.
  4. Investigate: preserve VPN logs and correlate suspicious sessions with management, virtualization and backup access. Examine new privileged access and control changes in particular.
  5. Validate: check fresh legitimate sessions and allowed versus blocked access from the VPN zone using test accounts and destinations. Record the version, policy and remaining exceptions.

What to keep in mind

An updated VPN and a closed incident are different outcomes. Confirm the fix and separately investigate access that may have occurred before the change.
