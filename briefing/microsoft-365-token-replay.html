Stolen Microsoft 365 sessions: why a password reset is not enough

Token replay and identity persistence: review sessions, devices, MFA methods and email rules.

weownit.net editorial analysis · weownit.net publication: 2026-10-26 · Reviewed: 2026-10-26

Topic

Microsoft 365 · Entra ID

Original source

Huntress ↗ · Microsoft Learn — Revoke access ↗ · Microsoft Learn — Token Protection ↗

In focus

Activity observed by researchers

What was reported

Huntress reports Microsoft 365 session replay and devices and MFA methods registered by attackers.

Applicability and impact

Business impact can include mailbox access, impersonation and fraud. Confirm activity in your own tenant; an unusual IP address or location alone does not establish session theft.

What to check and remediate

  1. Check: correlate interactive and non-interactive sign-ins with Entra and Exchange audit logs. Look for unexpected device registrations, MFA methods and inbox rules; retain exports and the reviewed time window.
  2. Contain: with the tenant administrator, revoke sessions and reset affected credentials; restrict the account if evidence warrants it. Verify the effect in applications because revocation is not instantaneous in every scenario.
  3. Remove persistence: after preserving evidence, remove unauthorized devices, MFA methods, forwarding rules and suspicious delegated access.
  4. Prevent: pilot phishing-resistant MFA and Conditional Access. Evaluate Token Protection only for supported application, device and license combinations; do not assume universal coverage.
  5. Validate: confirm approved methods and a fresh legitimate sign-in; check old access and monitor for reappearing suspicious rules or devices. Use test accounts for control scenarios.
What to keep in mind

Closure covers both access and persistence. A password reset, MFA enrollment or new policy alone does not demonstrate that every attacker session has ended.
