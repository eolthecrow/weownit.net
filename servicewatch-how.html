How a review works

  1. Agree the scope

    Choose the domains and systems your team owns and approve the checks.

  2. Collect evidence

    Record which services respond and what they return at the time of the check.

  3. Choose an action

    Review the evidence with the system owner and decide what to keep, restrict or retest.

What the pilot checks

Individual checks have been tested in the private pilot. Coverage depends on the agreed scope.

Domains & DNS
See which approved names resolve and where their records point.
Reachable services
Identify which TCP ports answer from outside your network.
Web responses
See which websites and endpoints respond, redirect or return an error.
Certificates
Check the hostname and expiry date on the certificate a service presents.
Security settings
Flag specific responses, such as a publicly visible status page, for an engineer to verify.

Beyond the external review

Tested prototype

Exercise evidence

A reviewer selects findings from a synthetic exercise report before importing them into the workspace for follow-up.

Proposed

Threat intelligence

Compare an approved domain or IP with threat reports and breach mentions. A reviewer would check the source and date of each match.

Proposed

Internal resilience exercises

A separately authorized lab exercise would show whether network boundaries and alerts detect or contain movement between systems.

Proposed

Continuous monitoring

Scheduled rechecks would highlight newly exposed services and notify the team when a previously reviewed asset changes.
